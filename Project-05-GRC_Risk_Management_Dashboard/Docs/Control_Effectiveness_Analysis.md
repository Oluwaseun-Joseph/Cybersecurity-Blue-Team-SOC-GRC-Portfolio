# Control Effectiveness Analysis

## Purpose
This document describes how control effectiveness is evaluated and how it
influences **residual risk levels** presented in the Risk Management Dashboard.

The objective is to demonstrate **risk reduction and governance awareness**, not
to claim full control implementation coverage.

---

## Control Context
The dashboard represents risk at a **conceptual control-impact level**, rather
than a detailed control inventory.

Controls are considered in broad categories, including:
- Preventive controls (e.g., access restrictions, security awareness)
- Detective controls (e.g., monitoring, logging)
- Corrective controls (e.g., incident response, remediation processes)

These controls may be technical, administrative, or procedural in nature.

---

## Control Effectiveness Assessment
Control effectiveness is evaluated qualitatively based on:
- Design adequacy (whether the control addresses the risk)
- Consistency of application
- Ongoing oversight and monitoring

Effectiveness is expressed using qualitative ratings such as:
- Low
- Medium
- High

These ratings are used to **inform residual risk**, not to produce an audit-grade
control assessment.

---

## Relationship Between Controls and Residual Risk
Residual risk represents the level of risk **remaining after controls are
considered**.

Control effectiveness influences residual risk by reducing:
- The likelihood that a risk event occurs
- The impact severity if the event materializes
- Or both

This relationship is reflected in the dashboard through comparison of
**Inherent Risk versus Residual Risk** values.

---

## Observations from the Dashboard
Analysis of the dashboard indicates that:
- Risks with stronger control assumptions show measurable reductions from
  inherent to residual risk
- Risks with weaker or partially effective controls retain higher residual risk
- Residual risk highlights areas where additional controls or improvements may
  be required

Residual risk therefore serves as a **decision-support indicator**, not a
guarantee of risk elimination.

---

## Governance and Continuous Improvement
Control effectiveness should be reviewed periodically to:
- Address changes in threat landscape
- Reflect organizational or system changes
- Improve alignment between controls and risk exposure

This approach supports continuous risk management and aligns with governance
best practices outlined in frameworks such as NIST and ISO.
